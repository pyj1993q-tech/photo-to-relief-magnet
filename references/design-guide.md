# Design Guide

Use this reference when the source image does not map cleanly to a physical magnet structure, or when several outputs must form a consistent series.

## Structure Selection

| Source image | Preferred structure | Why | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pet, person, single flower, simple object | Die-cut silhouette | Keeps the subject immediate and collectible | Weak legs, whiskers, straps, stems, or other fragile projections |
| Pet or character needing more narrative | Plaque with break-the-frame overlap | Adds depth and story without losing one connected body | Decorative cliches, excessive props, identity drift |
| Bouquet or branching plant | Connected die-cut or compact plaque | Preserves organic contour while supporting thin elements | Floating leaves, isolated petals, tangled negative space |
| Landscape, building, street, garden | Rounded framed plaque | Retains the full composition and gives the relief a stable edge | Looking like a flat photo pasted into a frame |
| Dense flowers or repeating texture | Simple rounded square/rectangle | Lets color rhythm and layered carving carry the design | Empty filler background, over-simplification |
| Memorial text or signage | Framed plaque with shallow relief | Supports exact text and architectural context | Misspelling, invented text, unreadable lettering |

## Relief Hierarchy

Describe no more depth layers than the image needs:

- **High relief:** hero subject, face, paws, signature flower, or primary architectural feature.
- **Middle relief:** supporting foliage, clothing, branches, props, or facade details.
- **Low relief/recessed:** sky, abstract color field, distant architecture, engraved patterns, or text bed.
- **Raised rim/base:** the connected physical support and clean product boundary.

Depth should improve legibility. It must not turn the concept into a freestanding figurine or create impossible undercuts.

## Preservation and Simplification

Preserve the few cues that carry identity: silhouette, proportions, pose, expression, signature colors, landmark outline, composition, or meaningful text. Simplify noise that does not carry the memory: unrelated people, shop signs, QR codes, advertising cards, random floor objects, clutter, and incidental reflections.

Do not call a creative relief interpretation a strict reconstruction. State where details were simplified for readability.

## Series System

Lock a small set of shared variables:

- satin-matte painted resin;
- a restrained creamy-white rim or base when appropriate;
- consistent studio lighting and shadow softness;
- similar presentation margins and camera angle;
- related, source-led color grading;
- comparable relief depth and edge roundness.

Do not force every subject into the same silhouette. Consistency comes from material and presentation as much as shape.

## Presentation Versus Production

A white or colored canvas around the object is presentation space, not material. A soft contact shadow is a display cue, not part of a cut path. A rendered checkerboard is only a pattern.

Concept art can inform later modeling, but it does not prove wall thickness, magnet placement, minimum feature size, draft angle, tooling feasibility, color separation, or printability.

When the user intends to manufacture the design, request the supplier's actual dimensions, minimum line width, thickness, bleed, color, material, magnet, and file-format requirements. Do not infer those values from the concept render.

## Factual Boundaries

- Keep people and pets recognizable; do not quietly change identity.
- Preserve landmark-defining shapes and exact memorial wording.
- Remove unrelated people, wires, QR codes, advertising cards, random tools, and repeated foliage only when doing so does not erase a memory cue.
- Do not invent logos, model numbers, awards, place names, packaging, or inscriptions.
- If a generation service refuses a subject, report the observed refusal and acknowledge that the cause may be unknown. Offer an original alternative only after the user agrees to change the subject.
